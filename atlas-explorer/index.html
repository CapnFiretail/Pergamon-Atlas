What is the Pergamon Atlas?

A permanent, infinite archive — every artifact has a fixed location, a permanent address, and an identity that can never be duplicated.

The Premise

The Pergamon Atlas is an infinite archival space. Every page on this site — every tool, every game, every artifact — exists simultaneously as a webpage and as a fixed point in a vast coordinate system. No artifact is at the center. No artifact is more central than any other. The Atlas simply extends in all directions without boundary.

When you navigate here, you are not browsing a website in the conventional sense. You are visiting artifacts. Each one occupies a permanent location, has a permanent identity, and can be reached from any other artifact by traversing the space between them.

Coordinate Space

The Atlas spans ±16,777,215 units on each of three axes — roughly 37.7 sextillion distinct positions. Artifact positions are assigned deterministically by hashing each artifact's URL path, so every location is permanent and reproducible. Artifacts do not cluster by type; proximity in the Atlas is pure spatial chance.

In practice, any two artifacts are tens of millions of units apart. The Atlas is not crowded. It is vast by design.

Atlas Addresses

Every artifact is assigned an Atlas Address: an encoded string that encodes its exact position. Addresses take the form:

ATLAS-BKCM2-NR4QF-WVTJ8

The three segments each encode one axis using a base-32 alphabet that excludes visually ambiguous characters (no O, I, 0, or 1). The encoding is XOR-scrambled using the Pergamon Constant, ensuring nearby coordinates produce very different-looking addresses. An address can always be decoded back to exact coordinates.

The Pergamon Constant

The Atlas is shaped by its own name. The Pergamon Constant is the hexadecimal encoding of the word "PERGAMON" in ASCII:

Pergamon Constant
0x50455247414D4F4E
P · E · R · G · A · M · O · N

This constant is used as the scrambling key throughout the address system — encoding coordinates, scrambling position data, seeding core computations. The Atlas is not shaped by an arbitrary number. It is shaped by its own identity.

This reflects a principle the Atlas is built around: the phenomenon of reference points. In physics, there is no absolute reference frame — every position is defined relative to others, and none is privileged. The Pergamon Constant does not mark a center. It is the medium through which the space is measured.

Artifact Identity

Each artifact carries two forms of identity. The Atlas Address encodes where it is. The Artifact Code encodes when it was registered — a fingerprint derived from its creation date, time, and path, combined with a dynamic segment from its current address. If the Atlas ever expands and positions shift, the dynamic segment updates while the creation fingerprint remains fixed.

Navigation

At the bottom of every artifact page is the Atlas Reference panel. It shows the artifact's identity and options to move through the Atlas.

  • Explore Nearby — the nearest artifact in each of six directions from your current position.
  • Jump to Address — enter any valid Atlas Address to travel directly to that artifact.
  • Random Jump — generate a random position and resolve to the nearest artifact.
  • View Catalogs — see where this artifact is formally cataloged.

Archives

Some artifacts are archived. They remain in the Atlas — coordinates and addresses are permanent — but they are marked as compressed. Archived artifacts do not display the Atlas Reference panel and are not listed as neighbors. They exist in the Atlas as silent, fixed points. They do not disappear.

The Atlas is not a map. It is the space the map describes.
