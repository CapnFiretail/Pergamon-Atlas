Pergamon Atlas

What is the Pergamon Atlas?

A permanent archive without a center
The Premise

The Pergamon Atlas is an infinite archival space. Every page on this site — every tool, every game, every artifact — exists simultaneously as a webpage and as a fixed point in a vast coordinate system. No artifact is at the center. No artifact is more central than any other. The Atlas simply extends in all directions without boundary.

When you navigate here, you are not browsing a website in the conventional sense. You are visiting artifacts. Each one occupies a permanent location, has a permanent identity, and can be reached from any other artifact by traversing the space between them.

Coordinate Space

The Atlas spans ±16,777,215 units on each of three axes. This yields roughly 37.7 sextillion distinct positions — far more than this site will ever need. Positions are assigned deterministically by hashing each artifact's path, so every artifact's location is permanent and reproducible from its URL alone.

Artifacts do not cluster by type. A tool and a game are equally likely to be neighbors in the Atlas. Proximity in the Atlas does not imply meaning — it is pure spatial chance, like stars that appear near each other in the sky but exist in no real relation.

Atlas Addresses

Every artifact is assigned an Atlas Address: a permanent, encoded string that encodes its exact position in the coordinate space. Addresses take the form:

ATLAS-BKCM2-NR4QF-WVTJ8

The three segments each encode one axis (X, Y, Z) using a base-32 alphabet that excludes visually ambiguous characters (no O, I, 0, or 1). The encoding is XOR-scrambled using the Pergamon Constant, ensuring that nearby coordinates produce very different-looking addresses.

An address can be decoded back to exact coordinates. This means knowing an address is sufficient to locate any artifact precisely — even an artifact that doesn't exist yet. The address space is the Atlas; artifacts merely occupy points within it.

The Pergamon Constant

The Atlas is shaped by its own name. The Pergamon Constant is the hexadecimal encoding of the word "PERGAMON" in ASCII:

Pergamon Constant
0x50455247414D4F4E
P · E · R · G · A · M · O · N

This constant is used as the scrambling key throughout the address system. It encodes the coordinate axes, scrambles position data, and seeds certain computations. The Atlas is not shaped by an arbitrary number — it is shaped by its own identity.

This reflects a deeper principle the Atlas is built around: the phenomenon of reference points. In physics, there is no absolute reference frame. Every position is defined relative to other positions, and no position is privileged. The Pergamon Atlas operates the same way. The constant does not mark the center. It is simply the medium through which the space is measured.

Artifact Identity

Each artifact carries two forms of identity. The Atlas Address encodes where it is. The Artifact Code encodes when and under what conditions it was first registered — a fixed fingerprint of its creation moment, combined with a dynamic segment derived from its current address.

If the Atlas ever expands — if space between artifacts is stretched to make room for new ones — an artifact's address may change to reflect its new position. The artifact code records what was true at the moment of creation, while the address records what is true now. Both together form a complete identity.

Navigation

At the bottom of every artifact page you will find the Atlas Reference panel. It shows the artifact's identity, its nearest neighbors in the coordinate space, and options to jump to any address directly or travel to a random position. Catalogs show all the places a given artifact is formally listed.

  • Explore Nearby — artifacts closest to the six cardinal directions from your current position.
  • Jump to Address — enter any valid Atlas Address to travel directly to that artifact.
  • Random Jump — generate a random position and resolve to the nearest artifact.
  • View Catalogs — see where this artifact is formally cataloged.
Archives

Some artifacts are archived. They remain in the Atlas — their coordinates and addresses are permanent — but they are marked as compressed. Archived artifacts do not display the Atlas Reference panel and are not listed as neighbors. They exist in the Atlas as silent, fixed points. They do not disappear.

The Atlas is not a map. It is the space the map describes.
